## Project Overview

<div class="project-overview">
Twenty years after its Broadway debut, Wicked remains a streaming phenomenon. But what separates the most-played tracks from the forgotten ones? I set out to answer this using **real Spotify data** from 28 tracks, armed with statistics and a healthy skepticism of conventional wisdom.

Spoiler alert: Almost everything the music industry tells you about "successful songs" is backwards.

Using **correlation analysis, ANOVA, K-means clustering, and PCA**, I analyzed actual Spotify metrics to understand what makes certain songs resonate. The plan was simple: prove that shorter songs dominate streaming, that popularity follows predictable patterns, and that "Defying Gravity" reigns supreme.

Reality had other plans.

Turns out "For Good" wins, duration doesn't matter even slightly (r=0.05, p=0.80), and the data reveals three distinct song archetypes that nobody talks about. Sometimes the best research findings are the ones that make you rethink everything you thought you knew.
</div>

## What The Data Actually Showed

<div class="insight-box">

### The Winner Nobody Expected (Sorry, Elphaba)
"For Good," the emotional duet near the finale, scores highest in Spotify popularity (58/100), edging out crowd favorites like "Defying Gravity" (56) and "No Good Deed" (57). Apparently listeners prefer crying to their morning coffee over belting high notes. Who knew?
</div>

<div class="insight-box">

### Duration Doesn't Matter (And I Have Receipts)
Correlation between song length and popularity? A measly **r=0.05 with p=0.80**. Translation: whether a song runs 50 seconds or 7.6 minutes has *zero* predictive power for streaming success. ANOVA confirms it (F=1.76, p=0.19). The "keep it short for attention spans" advice? Statistically baseless-at least for this soundtrack.
</div>

<div class="insight-box">

### Three Song Archetypes Nobody Talks About
K-means clustering (k=3) reveals distinct groups: **Epic Numbers** (long, popular showstoppers), **Deep Cuts** (short, less-streamed but plot-essential tracks), and **Solid Mid-Tier** (balanced popularity and length). It's not a hierarchy. Each serves a different purpose. The soundtrack works *because* it has variety, not despite it.
</div>

I collected data on 28 tracks from the Wicked soundtrack using Spotify's Web API, capturing track metadata (name, duration, popularity scores) and analyzing patterns in listener engagement. The goal was simple: understand what makes certain songs succeed while others fade into the background.

The approach: descriptive statistics, correlation analysis, hypothesis testing (ANOVA, t-tests), unsupervised learning (K-means clustering, PCA), and interactive visualizations. No simulated data, no assumptions. Just real numbers from real listeners.

**Bottom Line:** The quiet moments win. People return to songs that make them feel something real, not just songs that make them want to sing along.

### Duration vs. Popularity: The Surprising Non-Relationship

```{python}
#| echo: false
#| eval: true
#| warning: false

import plotly.graph_objects as go
import json
import numpy as np
from scipy import stats

# Load real data
with open('/Users/isfarbaset/Documents/wicked-tiktok-analysis/data/processed/viz_data.json', 'r') as f:
    data = json.load(f)

scatter_data = data['scatter']

# Filter out tracks with 0 popularity (international versions not on main platform)
filtered = [s for s in scatter_data if s['popularity'] > 0]

songs = [s['clean_name'] for s in filtered]
duration = [s['duration_min'] for s in filtered]
popularity = [s['popularity'] for s in filtered]

# Calculate correlation
corr, p_value = stats.pearsonr(duration, popularity)

# Size markers by popularity
sizes = [p * 1.2 for p in popularity]

fig = go.Figure()

fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(
    x=duration,
    y=popularity,
    mode='markers',
    marker=dict(
        size=sizes,
        color=popularity,
        colorscale=[[0, '#E91E8C'], [0.5, '#FFD700'], [1, '#00A651']],
        showscale=True,
        colorbar=dict(
            title=dict(text='Popularity', font=dict(size=11)),
            tickfont=dict(size=10)
        ),
        line=dict(color='#1a1a1a', width=1),
        opacity=0.8
    ),
    text=songs,
    hovertemplate='<b>%{text}</b><br>Duration: %{x:.2f} min<br>Popularity: %{y}<extra></extra>'
))

# Add trendline
z = np.polyfit(duration, popularity, 1)
p = np.poly1d(z)
x_line = np.linspace(min(duration), max(duration), 100)
y_line = p(x_line)

fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(
    x=x_line,
    y=y_line,
    mode='lines',
    line=dict(color='#8B5CF6', width=2, dash='dash'),
    name=f'Trend (r={corr:.3f}, p={p_value:.3f})',
    hoverinfo='skip'
))

fig.update_layout(
    title=dict(
        text=f'Duration vs. Popularity: Weak Correlation (r={corr:.3f})',
        font=dict(size=18, color='#1a1a1a', family='Segoe UI', weight='bold')
    ),
    xaxis=dict(
        title=dict(text='Song Duration (minutes)', font=dict(size=12)),
        showgrid=True,
        gridcolor='#f0f0f0'
    ),
    yaxis=dict(
        title=dict(text='Spotify Popularity Score', font=dict(size=12)),
        showgrid=True,
        gridcolor='#f0f0f0'
    ),
    plot_bgcolor='white',
    paper_bgcolor='white',
    height=500,
    showlegend=True,
    legend=dict(
        x=0.02,
        y=0.98,
        bgcolor='rgba(255,255,255,0.9)',
        bordercolor='#1a1a1a',
        borderwidth=1
    ),
    font=dict(family='Segoe UI', size=12, color='#333')
)

fig.show()
```

::: {.insight-box}
**Statistical Reality Check**: With a correlation coefficient of just **r=0.05** and **p=0.80**, there's no statistically significant relationship between song duration and popularity. Duration explains less than 0.3% of variance in popularity (r²=0.0025).

**What this demolishes:** The entire "TL;DR generation needs 2-minute songs" narrative. "Dancing Through Life" clocks in at 7.62 minutes and sits comfortably in the top 10. Long-form content wins when it's actually *good*. Revolutionary concept, I know.

**Bottom Line:** Song length doesn't matter. If people love it, they'll listen to all 7 minutes. If they don't, they'll skip it at 30 seconds. Quality > runtime.
:::

### K-Means Clustering: Three Song Archetypes

```{python}
#| echo: false
#| eval: true
#| warning: false

import plotly.graph_objects as go
import json

# Load real data
with open('/Users/isfarbaset/Documents/wicked-tiktok-analysis/data/processed/viz_data.json', 'r') as f:
    data = json.load(f)

clusters = data['clusters']

cluster_names = ['Epic Showstoppers', 'International Variants', 'Solid Mid-Tier']
avg_pop = [c['avg_popularity'] for c in clusters]
avg_dur = [c['avg_duration'] for c in clusters]
counts = [c['count'] for c in clusters]

colors_cluster = ['#00A651', '#8B5CF6', '#E91E8C']

fig = go.Figure()

fig.add_trace(go.Bar(
    name='Avg Popularity',
    x=cluster_names,
    y=avg_pop,
    marker=dict(color=colors_cluster, line=dict(color='#1a1a1a', width=1)),
    text=[f'{v:.1f}' for v in avg_pop],
    textposition='outside',
    textfont=dict(size=12, weight='bold'),
    yaxis='y',
    offsetgroup=1,
    hovertemplate='<b>%{x}</b><br>Avg Popularity: %{y:.1f}<extra></extra>'
))

fig.add_trace(go.Bar(
    name='Avg Duration (min)',
    x=cluster_names,
    y=avg_dur,
    marker=dict(
        color=colors_cluster,
        line=dict(color='#1a1a1a', width=1),
        pattern=dict(shape='/', fgcolor='white', solidity=0.4)
    ),
    text=[f'{v:.1f}' for v in avg_dur],
    textposition='outside',
    textfont=dict(size=12, weight='bold'),
    yaxis='y2',
    offsetgroup=2,
    hovertemplate='<b>%{x}</b><br>Avg Duration: %{y:.2f} min<extra></extra>'
))

fig.update_layout(
    title=dict(
        text='Song Clusters: Popularity vs. Duration Patterns',
        font=dict(size=18, color='#1a1a1a', family='Segoe UI', weight='bold')
    ),
    xaxis=dict(
        title='',
        tickfont=dict(size=11, family='Segoe UI'),
        showgrid=False
    ),
    yaxis=dict(
        title=dict(text='Avg Popularity Score', font=dict(size=12)),
        showgrid=True,
        gridcolor='#f0f0f0',
        range=[0, 70]
    ),
    yaxis2=dict(
        title=dict(text='Avg Duration (minutes)', font=dict(size=12)),
        overlaying='y',
        side='right',
        showgrid=False,
        range=[0, 10]
    ),
    barmode='group',
    plot_bgcolor='white',
    paper_bgcolor='white',
    height=450,
    legend=dict(
        orientation="h",
        yanchor="top",
        y=-0.12,
        xanchor="center",
        x=0.5,
        font=dict(size=11)
    ),
    margin=dict(b=80, t=60),
    font=dict(family='Segoe UI', size=12, color='#333')
)

# Add annotations for cluster sizes
for i, (name, count) in enumerate(zip(cluster_names, counts)):
    fig.add_annotation(
        x=i,
        y=65,
        text=f'n={count} tracks',
        showarrow=False,
        font=dict(size=10, color='#666', family='Segoe UI'),
        bgcolor='rgba(255,255,255,0.8)',
        bordercolor='#ddd',
        borderwidth=1,
        borderpad=4
    )

fig.show()
```

::: {.insight-box}
**Cluster Insight**: K-means analysis (k=3) reveals three distinct song types that actually make sense:

- **Cluster 0: Epic Showstoppers** - Long (avg 6.18 min), popular (avg 50.6), the tracks people queue up specifically
- **Cluster 1: International Variants** - Low/zero popularity on main Spotify (foreign language versions serve different markets)
- **Cluster 2: Solid Mid-Tier** - Shorter (avg 2.69 min), balanced popularity (avg 47.9), the narrative glue holding it all together

**What this reveals:** Successful soundtracks aren't monoliths. They need peaks *and* valleys. The "filler tracks" aren't filler-they're structural. Remove them and the whole thing collapses. It's data-driven vindication for every "I'm Not That Girl (Reprise)" out there.

**Bottom Line:** Great albums need variety. You can't have all bangers-the quiet moments make the big moments matter. The "boring" tracks aren't mistakes; they're part of the design.
:::

### PCA Visualization: The Hidden Structure

```{python}
#| echo: false
#| eval: true
#| warning: false

import plotly.graph_objects as go
import pandas as pd

# Load the full analysis results with PCA coordinates
df = pd.read_csv('/Users/isfarbaset/Documents/wicked-tiktok-analysis/data/processed/spotify_analysis_results.csv')

# Filter for clarity (remove 0-popularity tracks)
df_filtered = df[df['popularity'] > 0].copy()

# Clean track names
df_filtered['clean_name'] = df_filtered['track_name'].str.extract(r'^"?([^"]+?)"?\s*-?\s*From')[0]
df_filtered['clean_name'] = df_filtered['clean_name'].fillna(df_filtered['track_name'].str.split(' - ').str[0])

# Color by cluster
cluster_colors = {0: '#00A651', 1: '#8B5CF6', 2: '#E91E8C'}
df_filtered['color'] = df_filtered['cluster'].map(cluster_colors)

fig = go.Figure()

for cluster_id in sorted(df_filtered['cluster'].unique()):
    cluster_data = df_filtered[df_filtered['cluster'] == cluster_id]
    cluster_names = ['Epic Showstoppers', 'International', 'Mid-Tier']
    
    fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(
        x=cluster_data['PC1'],
        y=cluster_data['PC2'],
        mode='markers+text',
        name=cluster_names[cluster_id],
        marker=dict(
            size=cluster_data['popularity'] * 0.8,
            color=cluster_colors[cluster_id],
            line=dict(color='#1a1a1a', width=1),
            opacity=0.7
        ),
        text=cluster_data['clean_name'],
        textposition='top center',
        textfont=dict(size=8, family='Segoe UI'),
        hovertemplate='<b>%{text}</b><br>PC1: %{x:.2f}<br>PC2: %{y:.2f}<br>Popularity: %{marker.size:.0f}<extra></extra>'
    ))

fig.update_layout(
    title=dict(
        text='PCA: Songs in 2D Feature Space (Colored by Cluster)',
        font=dict(size=18, color='#1a1a1a', family='Segoe UI', weight='bold')
    ),
    xaxis=dict(
        title=dict(text='Principal Component 1 (Duration + Popularity)', font=dict(size=12)),
        showgrid=True,
        gridcolor='#f0f0f0',
        zeroline=True,
        zerolinecolor='#999',
        zerolinewidth=2
    ),
    yaxis=dict(
        title=dict(text='Principal Component 2 (Duration vs. Popularity)', font=dict(size=12)),
        showgrid=True,
        gridcolor='#f0f0f0',
        zeroline=True,
        zerolinecolor='#999',
        zerolinewidth=2
    ),
    plot_bgcolor='white',
    paper_bgcolor='white',
    height=550,
    showlegend=True,
    legend=dict(
        x=0.02,
        y=0.98,
        bgcolor='rgba(255,255,255,0.9)',
        bordercolor='#1a1a1a',
        borderwidth=1,
```

Analysis Steps: 1. Descriptive Statistics - Computed mean, median, std, range for all metrics 2. Correlation Analysis - Pearson correlation between duration and popularity 3. ANOVA - Tested duration differences across popularity tiers 4. T-Tests - Compared long vs. short songs (median split) 5. K-Means Clustering - Identified song archetypes (k=3) 6. PCA - Reduced feature space to 2D for visualization

Technical Learnings

API Integration: Working with Spotify’s Web API taught me the importance of rate limiting, error handling, and data validation. Not every API call succeeds-building in retry logic and graceful failures is essential.

Statistical Storytelling: Raw test results (r=0.05, p=0.80) mean nothing without interpretation. The real skill is translating statistical jargon into insights a non-technical audience can understand and act on.

Visualization Choices: Interactive charts > static images. Plotly’s hover effects and zoom capabilities let users explore the data themselves, increasing engagement and understanding.

Knowing When to Stop: I could’ve added more features, more tests, more models. But at some point, you’re not adding insight-you’re just adding complexity. The best analysis is the one that answers the question clearly, not the one that throws every tool at the problem.

The Real Insight

Data science can tell you what happened. With enough data, it can even tell you how it happened. But the why-the cultural moments, the emotional connections, the reasons “For Good” resonates more than “Defying Gravity”-that requires human interpretation.

This project taught me that the best analysis acknowledges its limitations. The numbers got us 80% of the way there. The other 20%? That’s where the story lives.

What’s Next: Future Directions

If I were to extend this analysis or build something similar:

1. Audio Feature Deep-Dive
Spotify provides 10+ audio features (danceability, energy, valence, tempo, acousticness, etc.). Analyzing how these correlate with popularity could reveal why certain songs succeed beyond just duration.

2. Time-Series Analysis
Track how popularity scores change over time. Do songs gain popularity post-movie release? Do certain tracks spike during award season or viral moments?

3. Cross-Soundtrack Comparison
How does Wicked compare to Hamilton, Dear Evan Hansen, or Six? Are the patterns universal across Broadway soundtracks, or is each unique?

4. Sentiment Analysis
Mine YouTube comments, Reddit discussions, and social media to understand why people connect with certain songs. Qualitative data could fill in the gaps quantitative analysis misses.

5. Predictive Modeling
Build a model to predict which tracks from new musicals will become streaming hits. Won’t be perfect (nothing is), but could help marketing teams prioritize promotion.

6. Lyrical Analysis
Use NLP to analyze song lyrics-word frequency, sentiment, complexity. Do simpler lyrics correlate with higher popularity? Do emotional themes matter?
